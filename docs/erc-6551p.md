## Abstract

This proposal extends [ERC-6551](./eip-6551.md) so that an NFT that owns an account can change hands without a loss to
the buyer. It has three parts. **The account**: only the NFT's current owner can make it call; the owner is checked
again after every call of a batch; no grant that the account can read back outlives the batch that made it; the
account gives no NFT approval, no NFT operator and no signature; a fixed list of rights with no read-back is refused.
**The collection**: a transfer made by anyone other than the owner passes only while the account is not inside a batch,
the seller is not an ERC-6551 account, and either the token was made ready in an earlier transaction and its account
has not acted since, or a one-use ticket of a named market or zone matches the transfer and the account's state.
**The checked sale**: the buyer writes conditions on the account (balances, positions, a state value), and they are
checked after the NFT transfer, in the same transaction; a failed condition reverts the fill. A profile for Seaport
1.6 zones is included. No lock, freeze or escrow is used.

## Motivation

ERC-6551 gives every NFT an account and leaves the sale out of scope: "Preventing fraud is outside the scope of this
proposal." Its author gives the open sale question as the reason the ERC is not Final (Ethereum Magicians thread
13030, post 225: "How can marketplaces efficiently prevent bait-and-switch scams involving TBAs? … they still have
edge cases and have not been fully proven"). The issues, with their public sources, are listed in
`problem.md`:

1. **The last-second drain.** The seller empties the account just before the buyer's fill (ERC-6551, Fraud
   Prevention).
2. **Leftover approvals and operators.** An allowance or operator that the account gave before the sale still works
   after it, and it does not change `state()` (thread posts 81, 182, 188, 206).
3. **Signatures.** ERC-6551 requires ERC-1271 signature validation. A permit or an off-chain order signed for the
   account moves value through another contract without a change of `state()`.
4. **The lock.** A lock freezes the account, so an agent that lives in it stops; a lock also does not stop
   "transfers using allowances" and "introduced a griefing mechanism" (thread post 237).
5. **An owner change inside a batch.** Later legs of a batch run with an authority that the NFT's new owner holds.
6. **Ownership cycles and nested accounts.** A token inside its own account (or a deeper loop) locks the assets for
   good; an account that holds another account's NFT can change who controls it without a transfer of that NFT.

The thread states the first two issues in the author's words: "The edge case here is approval-based transfers, which
would not change account state" (post 206), and a lock "introduced a griefing mechanism" (post 237). The same two
defects were found again in 2026 in the review of a sibling proposal (ERC-8216 thread, posts 2 and 3): a drain through
`execute`, and "a third party standing approval rights … sell the parent NFT, then the approved address drains
everything after delivery".

NFTs whose account holds the working capital of an AI agent make these issues sharp: the account is active while the
NFT is listed, and its value is in what it holds. This proposal closes 1–5 on a stated surface, closes cycles inside
one collection (6), and names what stays outside it (Security Considerations).

## Specification

The key words "MUST", "MUST NOT", "REQUIRED", "SHALL", "SHALL NOT", "SHOULD", "SHOULD NOT", "RECOMMENDED", "NOT
RECOMMENDED", "MAY", and "OPTIONAL" in this document are to be interpreted as described in RFC 2119 and RFC 8174.

Terms: a **batch** is one call of `execute` or `executeBatch` on the account; a **leg** is one call that a batch makes;
a **grant** is a right of a third party over the account's holdings (an allowance, an operator, a delegation); a
**modeled surface** is a grant that the account can decode and read back (§1.7); the **canonical account** of a token
is the registry clone of the collection's account implementation with salt 0 on this chain. Anyone can create other
accounts for the same token (another salt or implementation); this proposal speaks only of the canonical account.

### 1. The account

An ERC-6551P account is an ERC-6551 account (an ERC-1167 clone made by the canonical ERC-6551 registry, with the
standard footer) that implements `IERC6551Account`, `IERC6551Executable` and:

```solidity
/// @dev the ERC-165 identifier for this interface is `0x92ad233c`
interface IERC6551PAccount {
    /// @notice True while a batch of this account runs.
    function inBatch() external view returns (bool);
}
```

1. **Owner only.** `execute` and `executeBatch` MUST revert unless `msg.sender == owner()`, where `owner()` is
   `IERC721(tokenContract).ownerOf(tokenId)` when `chainId == block.chainid` and `address(0)` otherwise (a revert of
   `ownerOf` MUST give `address(0)`). The account MUST NOT have executors, permissions, overrides, plugins, modules,
   sessions, a guardian, an upgrade path or an ERC-4337 entry point.
2. **CALL only.** `operation` MUST be 0. Values 1 (DELEGATECALL), 2 (CREATE) and 3 (CREATE2) MUST revert. A leg MUST
   NOT target the account itself.
3. **One batch at a time.** The account MUST set a transient batch flag for the whole batch; `inBatch()` MUST return
   it; a nested `execute` or `executeBatch` MUST revert.
4. **State.** `state()` MUST change at the start of every batch, and MUST NEVER return a value that it returned
   before: readiness, tickets, the `STATE` condition and the ERC-5646 fingerprint all read "the same value" as "no
   batch since". A hash chain `state = keccak256(abi.encode(state, msg.data))` (the reference) or a strict counter
   meets this; `keccak256(msg.data)` alone does NOT (a seller drains, then repeats an old batch, and the old value
   comes back). A revert of the batch reverts the change. Deposits do not change it.
5. **The owner is checked after every leg.** After each leg, the account MUST revert the whole batch if `owner()` is
   not the owner at the start of the batch.
6. **No signatures.** `isValidSignature(bytes32,bytes)` MUST return a value other than `0x1626ba7e` for every input,
   and `isValidSigner(address,bytes)` MUST return a value other than `0x523e3260` for every input. The account MUST NOT
   answer `true` to `supportsInterface(0x1626ba7e)`.
7. **No modeled grant at rest.** For every leg (and every inner call of a known self-call wrapper, §1.8) whose selector
   is on the grant table below, the account MUST read the grant before the legs run and after they ran, and MUST
   revert if a grant is higher after the batch than before it. A read that reverts or returns short data MUST revert
   the batch. A grant used up inside the batch (approve, spend, reset) passes. A transient grant (ERC-7674) is a grant too: it
   lives until the end of the transaction, and a seller that is a contract can sell through a ticket path in the same
   transaction as its batch.

   | Surface | Write selectors | Read back | Rule |
   |---|---|---|---|
   | ERC-20 allowance | `approve(address,uint256)` on a target that is not NFT-like, `increaseAllowance`, `increaseApproval`, `decreaseAllowance`, `decreaseApproval`, ERC-1363 `approveAndCall` (both forms), ERC-7674 `temporaryApprove(address,uint256)`, DSToken `approve(address)` | `allowance(account, spender)` | post ≤ pre |
   | ERC-6909 / compare-and-set | `approve(address,uint256,uint256)`, `setOperator(address,bool)` | `allowance(account, spender, id)` and `allowance(account, spender)`; `isOperator(account, spender)` | the same getters answer before and after; post ≤ pre |
   | ERC-777 operator | `authorizeOperator(address)`, `revokeOperator(address)` | `isOperatorFor(operator, account)` | post ≤ pre |
   | Permit2 | `approve(address,address,uint160,uint48)` | `allowance(account, token, spender)` | a grant is live while `expiration >= block.timestamp` (Permit2 stores expiration 0 as now and refuses a spend only when `block.timestamp > expiration`); the live amount does not grow, and a live grant does not last longer |
   | Plain ERC-1155 operator | `setApprovalForAll(address,true)` on a target that answers ERC-165 `0xd9b67a26` true, `0x80ac58cd` false, `0xc26d96cc` false and has no `getApproved(uint256)` | `isApprovedForAll(account, operator)` | post ≤ pre |
   | Lending authorizations a deployment lists | for example Aave `approveDelegation`, Morpho `setAuthorization`, Compound III `allow` | their getters | post ≤ pre |

8. **Decoding.** Every decoded grant call MUST have exactly the ABI length of its selector and canonical words.
   The account MUST decode the self-call wrappers `multicall(bytes[])`, `multicall(uint256,bytes[])`,
   `multicall(bytes32,bytes[])` and `batch(bytes[],bool)`, recursively, and MUST revert a wrapper that is not
   canonically encoded. It MUST NOT scan raw calldata for words.
9. **No NFT approval and no NFT operator.** The account MUST revert `approve(a, v)` with `a != 0` on an NFT-like target
   (a target where `getApproved(v)` answers 32 bytes, or `supportsInterface(0x80ac58cd)` is true), and
   `setApprovalForAll(·, true)` on every target that is not a plain ERC-1155 as in §1.7. A revocation passes.
10. **Refused rights.** The account MUST revert a leg (at any decoded depth) whose selector is a grant with no
    read-back, and a leg to a target that stores rights it cannot read back. The minimum lists:
    - selectors: Seaport `validate`, CoW `setPreSignature(bytes,bool)`, Safe `approveHash(bytes32)`, ERC-1820
      `setManager(address,address)` and `setInterfaceImplementer(address,bytes32,address)`, Uniswap v4
      `subscribe(uint256,address,bytes)`, ERC-4907 `setUser(uint256,address,uint64)`, ERC-5006
      `createUserRecord(address,address,uint256,uint64,uint64)`, ERC-404 `erc721Approve(address,uint256)` and
      `erc20Approve(address,uint256)`, ERC-3525 `approve(uint256,address,uint256)` and `setApprovalForSlot`,
      delegate.xyz v1 and v2 delegation writes, `hope(address)`, Maker DssCdpManager `cdpAllow` and `urnAllow`,
      Balancer `setRelayerApproval(address,address,bool)`, Euler EVC `setAccountOperator`, `setOperator(bytes19,
      address,uint256)`, `call(address,address,uint256,bytes)` and `batch((address,address,uint256,bytes)[])`,
      Superfluid `setFlowrate`, `createFlow`, `grantPermissions`, `updateFlowOperatorPermissions` and the host's
      `callAgreement` and `batchCall`, the GDA forwarder's `distributeFlow` and the MacroForwarder's `runMacro` (a
      stream from the account keeps paying after the sale), Venus `updateDelegate(address,bool)` and dYdX
      `setOperators((address,bool)[])` (a delegate borrows or acts for the account), CryptoPunks `offerPunkForSale` and
      `offerPunkForSaleToAddress` and 0x v3 `preSign(bytes32)` (standing sale orders), 0x v4
      `registerAllowedOrderSigner`, Tokenbound `setPermissions`, `setOverrides` and `lock(uint256)` (keyed to the
      root owner, which stays this account across a sale), ERC-8004 `setAgentWallet` and `unsetAgentWallet` (the
      registry resets the agent's wallet link only when the identity token moves, and a sale does not move it);
    - targets: every canonical Seaport (1.1–1.6), the ERC-1820 registry, the delegate.xyz v1 and v2 registries, the
      MetaMask DelegationManager.

    A selector refusal holds only if no other entry point of the same target reaches the refused write with the
    account kept as the caller. So for every refused selector, a deployment MUST also refuse (or decode, as in §1.8)
    each self-call wrapper of that protocol (the EVC `call` / `batch` above are such wrappers), or refuse the
    protocol's address as a target on each chain. The lists are a minimum: a deployment adds every grant with no
    read-back of the protocols its accounts use, after a review of each protocol.
11. **Gas for the checks.** Each read of an untrusted target MUST be a STATICCALL with a fixed gas cap after a check
    that `gasleft()` leaves the whole cap (`cap × 64/63 + overhead`); the account MUST hold back the gas that its
    post-leg checks need. A call to the account therefore needs more gas than it uses (Security Considerations).
12. **Its own NFT.** `onERC721Received` MUST revert when the incoming token is the account's own token.
13. **The ready leg.** A leg that calls `readyToSell(uint256)` on the account's `tokenContract` MUST be the last leg,
    except that legs which cannot move value MAY follow it (a deployment names them; the reference implementation
    allows only one journal contract's `log(bytes)` with value 0). A `readyToSell` inside a wrapper MUST revert.
14. A batch that would leave a modeled grant, make a refused right, or lose the owner MUST revert as a whole.

### 2. The collection

An ERC-6551P collection is an ERC-721 whose every token owns an ERC-6551P account at the canonical registry, and
which implements [ERC-5646](./eip-5646.md) and:

```solidity
/// @dev the ERC-165 identifier for this interface is `0x6b7481e3`
interface IERC6551PCollection {
    event SaleReady(uint256 indexed id, address indexed owner, uint256 state);

    function readyToSell(uint256 id) external;
    function cancelReady(uint256 id) external;
    function saleReadyOf(uint256 id) external view returns (bool);
    function transferCount(uint256 id) external view returns (uint256);
    function openTicket(uint256 id, address from, address to) external;
    function ticketConsumed(uint256 id) external view returns (bool);
    function closeTicket(uint256 id) external;
    function MARKET() external view returns (address);
    function ZONE() external view returns (address);
}
```

1. **The sale rule.** For every transfer of `id` with `from != 0` that is made by an address other than `from` (an
   approved address or an operator), the collection MUST revert unless all of:
   - the token's account is not in a batch (`inBatch() == false`);
   - `from` is not an ERC-6551 account (RECOMMENDED test: its code is 173 bytes with the registry clone's header
     `363d3d373d3d3d363d73` and tail `5af43d82803e903d91602b57fd5bf3`, of any implementation);
   - either (a) **ready**: `readyToSell(id)` recorded `(from, account.state())` in an EARLIER transaction and the
     account's `state()` is unchanged; or (b) **ticket**: an open ticket of `id` matches `from`, `to` and the account's
     current `state()`; the transfer MUST consume it.

   A transfer by the owner itself and the mint are not subject to the rule. The collection MUST NOT move a holder's
   token by any other path that skips the rule (an internal transfer with no authorization check, an escrow, a rescue
   or staking function); in the reference kit the rule runs only when `auth != 0`, so an adopter that adds such a path
   loses the rule for it.
2. **Readiness.** `readyToSell(id)` MUST revert unless `msg.sender` is the token's account, and MUST revert when the
   owner is an ERC-6551 account. It records `keccak256(abi.encode(owner, account.state()))` and marks it as made in
   this transaction (transient). `saleReadyOf(id)` is true while the record matches the current owner and the
   account's current `state()` (also in the transaction of `readyToSell`, where a transfer on the ready path still
   reverts: an integrator that reads the view and fills in the same transaction gets `SaleNotReady`). `cancelReady(id)` (the owner or its account) ends readiness. Every transfer MUST end
   readiness. The end of readiness has no event of its own (a transfer emits `Transfer`; an account batch changes
   `state()`): an indexer reads `saleReadyOf(id)` again after either.
3. **Tickets.** `openTicket(id, from, to)` MUST revert unless `msg.sender` is `MARKET()` or `ZONE()`, and while a ticket
   of `id` is open. It stores `(from, to, opener, account.state())` in transient storage (EIP-1153). A ticket serves at
   most one transfer and never outlives the transaction. `ticketConsumed(id)` is true when the ticket served its
   transfer and the account's state equals the state at the opening. `closeTicket(id)` frees the slot and MUST revert
   unless the caller is the opener.
4. **Counts and fingerprint.** `transferCount(id)` MUST increase by one on every transfer with `from != 0` (also a
   transfer from the owner to itself). `getStateFingerprint(id)` (ERC-5646) MUST be `keccak256(abi.encode(ownerOf(id),
   transferCount(id), account.state()))`. Its scope is exactly "the same owner, no transfer and no account batch
   since"; it does NOT change on a deposit, on value that moves inside another protocol, or on a token admin action.
   ERC-5646 asks for all mutable state; a reader that needs the contents MUST use buyer conditions (§3.1).
5. **No nesting.** The collection MUST revert a transfer of a token into an existing ERC-6551 registry clone of ANY
   implementation bound to a token of the same collection on the same chain. An address with no code cannot be recognised
   (R-16).
6. **Immutables.** `MARKET()`, `ZONE()` and the account implementation MUST NOT change after deploy.
7. The collection MUST answer `supportsInterface` true for `0x6b7481e3` and for ERC-5646 (`0xf5112315`).

### 3. The checked sale

#### 3.1 Conditions

```solidity
struct Condition {
    uint8 kind;
    address target;
    uint256 id;
    uint256 min;
    bytes data;
}
```

A condition is read on the account `a` that the market or the zone **computes itself** from the sold id (the
canonical registry, its own immutable account implementation, salt 0, `block.chainid`, the collection, the id). A
market or zone therefore serves only collections whose account implementation is its own; for another collection the
conditions read a clone that the sale rule does not watch (front ends MUST check `accountImplementation()` against the
market's `ACCOUNT_IMPL()`). It
MUST require `a.code.length == 173`, `a.token() == (block.chainid, collection, soldId)` and `a.owner() == recipient`
after the transfer. Each read is a STATICCALL with a gas cap after the gas check of §1.11; a failed or malformed read
fails the condition.

| `kind` | Name | Holds when |
|---|---|---|
| 0 | `ETH` | `a.balance ≥ min` |
| 1 | `ERC20` | `IERC20(target).balanceOf(a) ≥ min` |
| 2 | `ERC721` | `IERC721(target).ownerOf(id) == a` and `getApproved(id) == 0` |
| 3 | `ERC1155` | `IERC1155(target).balanceOf(a, id) ≥ min` |
| 4 | `MINT_POSITION` (relative, OPTIONAL) | the collection's `positionOf(soldId) = p != 0`, and on the position manager `target`: `ownerOf(p) == a`, liquidity ≥ `min`, `getApproved(p) == 0`, no subscriber |
| 5 | `V4POSITION` | the same for position `id`, and the position's pool hook equals `abi.decode(data, (address))` |
| 6 | `ERC6909` | `IERC6909(target).balanceOf(a, id) ≥ min` |
| 7 | `IDENTITY` (relative, OPTIONAL) | the collection's `hasIdentity(soldId)`, and on the ERC-8004 registry `target`: `ownerOf(agentIdOf(soldId)) == a`, `getApproved` is 0, `getAgentWallet == a` |
| 8 | `STATE` | `a.state() == min` |
| 9 | `CALLEQ` | `staticcall(target, data)` returns exactly 32 bytes equal to `bytes32(min)` |
| 10 | `CALLGE` | the same 32 bytes, as `uint256`, `≥ min` |
| 11 | `CALLLE` | the same 32 bytes, as `uint256`, `≤ min` |

A kind not in the table MUST fail. The relative kinds read the collection; a collection that does not have
`positionOf` / `hasIdentity` / `agentIdOf` makes them fail.

**The buyer-conditions rule.** Every checked fill MUST carry at least one condition written by the buyer, and MUST
revert if one of them fails after the transfer. A seller's promise MAY be carried and checked too; it protects no one,
because a seller can write a promise that is always true. A fill MUST revert if a consideration item pays the sold
account. This rule is not a protection on its own: a seller can pay a forwarder that sends the buyer's money into the
account, so a balance condition holds on the buyer's own money. The buyer then pays the same total and gets what the
conditions name; front ends MUST NOT count money that the fill itself pays as value of the account.

#### 3.2 The market

A market serves every collection `c` with `c.MARKET() == address(market)`, checked on every call; it has no owner, no
fee and no setter, and holds no NFT and no buyer funds at rest. It MUST provide:

- `buy(Listing, bytes signature, Condition[] sellerPromise, Condition[] buyerConditions, address recipient)`: checks
  the seller's EIP-712 signature (EOA or ERC-1271 of the NFT OWNER's wallet, never the account), the expiry, the nonce,
  the salt, `transferCount(id) == listing.transferCount`, that the seller is not an ERC-6551 account; takes the payment;
  opens a ticket `(id, seller, recipient)`; transfers; requires `ticketConsumed(id)` and `ownerOf(id) == recipient`;
  checks the promise and the buyer's conditions; closes the ticket; pays the seller and the ERC-2981 royalty (capped by
  the signed `maxRoyalty`; a signer may sign 0, so the royalty is optional on the market; in `accept` only the buyer
  signs it, so the seller has no limit of its own and relies on the collection's cap).
- `accept(Offer, bytes signature, uint256 id, Condition[] buyerConditions, Condition[] sellerPromise)`: the owner of
  `id` fills a buyer's signed offer (`anyId` allowed); the same ticket and post-check; the buyer's conditions are bound
  by `conditionsHash` in the signed offer.
- `checkedFill(AdvancedOrder order, CriteriaResolver[] resolvers, bytes32 fulfillerConduitKey, Fill fill,
  Condition[] buyerConditions)` with `Fill = (address collection, uint256 soldId, address currency, uint256
  maxPayment)`: fills ANY Seaport 1.6 order whose offer is exactly the one sold NFT (order types 0–3, no criteria, one
  currency, nothing paid to the account, the offerer not an ERC-6551 account) with the market as the recipient, then
  sends the NFT to the caller and checks the buyer's conditions; refunds the change. It opens no ticket: a plain
  listing passes the collection's rule only while the token is ready; a zone order gets its ticket from the zone.

The listing type, as in the reference implementation:

```
Listing(address collection,uint256 id,uint256 transferCount,address seller,address currency,uint256 price,
        uint256 maxRoyalty,uint256 expiry,uint256 salt,bytes32 promiseHash,uint256 nonce)
Offer(address collection,uint256 id,bool anyId,address buyer,address currency,uint256 price,
      uint256 maxRoyalty,uint256 expiry,uint256 salt,bytes32 conditionsHash,uint256 nonce)
```

Events: `Sold(address indexed collection, uint256 indexed id, address indexed seller, address buyer, address
currency, uint256 price, uint256 royalty)`, `CheckedFill(address indexed collection, uint256 indexed id, address
indexed buyer, bytes32 orderHashOrZero)`.

#### 3.3 The Seaport 1.6 zone profile

A zone serves every collection with `c.ZONE() == address(zone)`. It MUST accept `authorizeOrder` and `validateOrder`
only from Seaport, MUST require exactly one sold item (`ERC721`, amount 1) and no consideration to the account it
computes, and MUST refuse an ERC-6551 account as the NFT's `from`.
- **Listing** (the NFT in `offer`): `zoneHash = keccak256(abi.encode(transferCount, promise))`, `extraData =
  abi.encode(promise, buyerConditions)` with non-empty `buyerConditions`; `authorizeOrder` requires the
  `transferCount` and opens `ticket(id, offerer, fulfiller)` (the recipient MUST be the fulfiller).
- **Offer** (the NFT in `consideration`, its recipient the offerer): `zoneHash = keccak256(abi.encode(conditions))`,
  `extraData = abi.encode(conditions)`, non-empty; opens `ticket(id, fulfiller, offerer)`.
- `validateOrder` requires the ticket consumed with the account state unchanged and `ownerOf(id)` equal to the bound
  `to`, checks every condition, and closes the ticket. A basic order (no `extraData`) is refused. Only order types 2–3
  call a zone.
- The zone MUST NOT call Seaport and MUST NOT be the offerer or the fulfiller of an order: Seaport skips both zone
  hooks when the caller is the zone, so such a call would fill a zone order with no ticket and no check.

## Rationale

- **Read-back instead of a lock.** A lock freezes the agent and does not stop allowances or signatures (thread post
  237). Reading every modeled grant before and after the batch lets the account act freely and still leave nothing at
  rest.
- **Refuse what cannot be read.** An NFT approval or an operator can make further rights (per-token approvals, rental
  users, subscriptions) that no read finds, and pre-signed orders and delegation registries keep rights the account
  cannot list. Refusing them is cheaper and simpler than modeling them. The cost is that the account itself does not
  trade NFTs on marketplaces; the owner's wallet does.
- **No signatures.** ERC-6551 allows an account to "invalidate the holder as a signer". With no signature, every
  movement of value is an `execute`, and every `execute` changes `state()`; readiness and tickets can then rely on
  `state()`.
- **The buyer's conditions, not the seller's promise.** Only a check that the buyer writes and that runs after the
  transfer protects the buyer; a seller can always write a promise that holds.
- **Two paths for a sale.** Readiness keeps ordinary marketplace listings working with no marketplace change (it gives
  G1, "the seller keeps no right", not G2, "you get what you checked"); tickets let the agent keep working while its NFT is
  listed on a checked path.
- **The seller is not an account.** Control of an account-held NFT can change without a transfer of that NFT, so no
  readiness or ticket can be trusted for it.
- **`transferCount`** kills a listing after a round trip of the token; it is also the ERC-5646 input.
- **No `surfaces()` getter.** The grant tables are code constants of a deployment; a getter that lists them is left to a
  later revision.

## Backwards Compatibility

- An ERC-6551P account is placed by the canonical ERC-6551 registry and answers `IERC6551Account` (`0x6faff5f1`) and
  `IERC6551Executable` (`0x51945447`); `execute` and `executeBatch` keep the AccountV3 ABI shapes.
- ERC-6551 says "All token bound account implementations MUST implement ERC-1271 signature validation" and that the
  holder "MUST be considered a valid signer" by default, while it "MAY implement additional authorization logic which
  invalidates the holder as a signer". An ERC-6551P account implements `isValidSignature` and `isValidSigner` and
  always rejects. Readers that require a valid ERC-1271 signature from the account (permits, off-chain orders, sign-in)
  do not work with it. It does not advertise ERC-1271 in ERC-165.
- `IERC6551Executable` says accounts "MUST accept" operations 0–3 and "MAY … restrict a signer's ability to execute
  certain operations"; an ERC-6551P account restricts operations to CALL.
- Tools built for Tokenbound AccountV3 (locks, overrides, permissions, ERC-4337) do not manage an ERC-6551P account.
- A collection keeps the ERC-721 interface. Marketplaces need no change for the ready path; a plain marketplace fill
  of a token that is not ready reverts.
- The account, the collection, the market and the zone read code with `EXTCODESIZE` / `EXTCODECOPY` (the clone footer,
  the seller test, the no-nesting test, `code.length == 173`); they need legacy (non-EOF) registry clones.
- The approve probe of §1.9 fails closed when a target's unknown-selector path uses all its gas. WETH9 (Ethereum,
  Base) has a payable fallback that writes state, so a STATICCALL of `getApproved` into it uses all gas: an account
  of the reference implementation cannot `approve` WETH9 on those chains (the 4663 WETH reverts cheaply and is not
  affected). A deployment on such a chain decides between this cost and a narrower probe rule.

## Security Considerations

The two guarantees are exactly these. **G1, the seller keeps no right**: after a transfer, the previous owner (and
anyone it named) holds no right over the account's own holdings on the modeled surfaces (§1.6–§1.10), on every path.
**G2, you get what you checked**: on a checked path (the market's `buy` / `accept`, `checkedFill`, a zone order), the
fill happens only if every buyer condition holds after the transfer. G2 does NOT hold on the plain ready path.

What stays outside them:

- **R-1 Undecoded grants.** A grant made through a call the account cannot decode (an unknown wrapper, a selector not
  in the table, a computed spender, a forwarder to another target, a target that lies or changes its getter inside the
  batch, an ERC-1155 extension whose operator can make further rights).
- **R-2 Bugs.** Implementations need an outside audit; the reference implementation has none yet.
- **R-3 The plain ready path gives no G2.** A seller can drain and make the token ready again in an earlier
  transaction before a plain fill (a listing filled directly, a plain offer that the seller fills). Front ends SHOULD
  fill listings through `checkedFill` and make offers through the market.
- **R-5 Built-in grants** that no account action made (a token with a hard-coded spender, and a contract that lets
  `Ownable(x).owner()` act for `x`: the account's `owner()` is the NFT holder, so such a contract lets the holder act
  for the account with no `execute` and no change of `state()`).
- **R-6 Configuration rights** not in the refused list (fee recipients, contract ownership, withdraw recipients, a
  pending owner of a two-step `transferOwnership`, an ERC20Votes `delegate`, a rewards receiver or claimer such as a
  Curve gauge `set_rewards_receiver` or an EigenLayer `setClaimerFor`): a `CALLEQ` on `owner()` does not see a
  pending owner, so a buyer who values such a contract checks `pendingOwner()` too. This includes **contracts that the
  account owns or co-owns** (a Safe, a DSProxy, a pool pair, any `Ownable`). For example, a Safe that has the account
  as an owner accepts the account's own call to `execTransaction` with no signature (Safe treats `msg.sender` as an
  approval), so the seller can add an owner, a module or a guard before the sale, and keep the Safe after it. The
  refusal of `approveHash` does not close this.
- **R-7 Value inside other protocols** moves under their rules (liquidation, fees, new debt).
- **R-8 NFTs of other collections with their own wallets** inside the account. The refusals of the Tokenbound
  `setPermissions`, `setOverrides` and `lock` writes stop only a direct call: an inner AccountV3 accepts DELEGATECALL in
  its own `execute`, so code that it runs can write the same storage.
- **R-9 Token admin powers** (blacklist, pause, rebase).
- **R-10 Owners whose control changes without a transfer** and that are not registry-clone accounts (a Safe owned by
  another NFT's account, an NFT-gated EIP-7702 delegate, an NFT-bound wallet that is not a registry clone), and a
  registry account that has no code at the sale (an account of another implementation can approve a conduit,
  pre-validate an order and self-destruct in the transaction that creates it, EIP-6780; the seller test reads code).
- **R-11 An owner-push sale** (the owner's own transfer to a contract that pays on receipt) has no check; such a buyer
  contract must check the account itself.
- **R-12 Dishonest collections.** A collection with false ERC-721 logic can name a real market or zone; front ends
  SHOULD list pinned collections only.
- **R-13 What conditions do not cover**: the value (not the amount) of a holding, a holding that cannot be read, a
  holding the front end does not find, a holding left out because the fill would exceed the gas limit (anyone can
  send tokens to any account), the tolerance share, a value that the seller can move inside a read (`CALLGE` on a
  price).
- **R-14 Other accounts of the same token.** Anyone can create more accounts for a token (another salt or
  implementation). The NFT owner controls them, their actions do not change the canonical `state()`, and an account of
  another implementation can keep approvals and signatures. The two guarantees cover only the canonical account;
  front ends show only that one (`test_K85_anotherAccountOfTheSameTokenIsOutsideThePromises`).
- **R-15 A cycle through another collection.** An owner can put a token of another collection into this token's
  account and then this token into that token's account (two owner transfers). Both accounts can then never act. It
  is a burn that the owner makes; no buyer loses on a sale path, because a seller that is an ERC-6551 account is
  refused (`test_K86_aCycleThroughAnotherCollectionIsAnOwnerMadeBurn`).
- **R-16 A token sent to the empty address of its own clone.** The clone of a token with another salt or implementation
  has no code until someone creates it, and no O(1) check recognises that address. An owner can send a token there. Once
  the clone exists, the token owns itself, and it and its account lock for good. It is a burn that the sender makes;
  nobody can force it on a holder (`test_K92_tokenSentToItsOwnEmptyCloneIsAnOwnerMadeBurn`). A front end SHOULD refuse a
  send to an address it can compute as such a clone.

(R-4 is not used; the numbers stay fixed so that references to them stay valid.)

**What the checked path does not add.** The buyer's conditions are read once, at the fill. A right outside G1 (R-1,
R-5, R-6, R-8) is used later, so it reaches a buyer on a checked path in the same way as on the plain path. A buyer
can check a known right with a `CALLEQ` condition on its getter (for example a rewards receiver, or the owners of a
known Safe), but only for a surface that the front end knows.

**Gas.** The account holds back gas for its checks after each leg, so a call needs more gas than it uses. Size the gas
limit with `eth_estimateGas`, never as "gas used × k". On a 4663 fork (2026-10-29, before the refusals of K-84 to K-95 were added) the needed limit was 1.65–2.24× the
gas used: a one-leg ETH send 120,356 / 65,530; a one-leg ERC-20 transfer 154,110 / 92,245; approve → sell → reset
403,473 / 179,980; `readyToSell` with a journal entry 179,674 / 108,694.

**Front ends.** A seller's promise can name any target (for example a fake position manager that answers every
read), so a front end MUST NOT show a promise as checked value; only the buyer's own conditions protect. A seller can make an account look rich with a worthless token, or answer a different balance to the
front end than to the market. Front ends SHOULD add a condition for every holding they find, label holdings whose
value they do not know, simulate the fill from the buyer's address before signing, and drop (and label) a holding whose
condition fails in simulation.

**Ordering.** The sequencer orders transactions; a seller can race the buyer. The checked paths make a race revert
the fill instead of draining the buyer.

**Readiness revives old plain listings.** Readiness is not bound to a listing. A transfer or an account batch only pauses a
plain listing: a seller who makes the token ready again makes every older plain listing of that owner fillable at its old
price, until that listing expires or is cancelled. A Sell flow SHOULD cancel the older orders it knows with Seaport `cancel`
before a new readiness. Seaport `incrementCounter` also works, but it ends every Seaport order of the wallet, for every NFT,
so it MUST be the seller's explicit choice. Market and zone listings bind `transferCount`, so a transfer ends them and
this revival does not reach them; they do not bind `state()`, so a seller who adds value to the account SHOULD cancel them.

**Check all, within the gas limit.** A front end that adds a condition for every holding can exceed the block gas
limit, because anyone can send dust tokens to any account. Front ends SHOULD cap the conditions per fill (measured gas
per kind), check the known holdings first, and label the holdings left out (R-13).

**A second implementation.** An implementation that follows only this text is safe on the paths above only if it
keeps every MUST, in particular: `state()` never repeats (§1.4); the owner is read after every leg (§1.5); every
modeled grant is read back and the refused lists are extended to the wrappers of each refused protocol (§1.7–§1.10);
the ready leg is last (§1.13); the market and the zone compute the account with their own implementation (§3.1). The
first tool-assisted review of this text (not an audit) found the §1.4, §1.10 and §1.13 gaps.
